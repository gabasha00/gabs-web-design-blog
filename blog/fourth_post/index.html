Reading Responses, Surveillance Capitalism

  • 04 March 2026

The Age of Surveillance Capitalism by Shosana Zuboff

When it comes to thinking about post-capitalism in relation to how it has affected the internet and its usage, this topic of surveillance is probably the topic I think about the most, so reading this passage was very insightful for me on that level. Post-capitalism in the age of internet surveillance has brought on a slew of greedy corporations and businesses that take our data and use it for…well, God knows what. The unprecedented power that corporations have over user data is really scary, and that notion of being in a time that is “unprecedented” is something Zuboff seems to really highlight in her writing. One thing I found interesting and which I hadn’t thought of before is how she brings up the fact that “when we encounter something unprecedented, we automatically interpret it through the lenses of familiar categories, thereby rendering invisible precisely that which is unprecedented (p. 13).” In regards to surveillance capitalism, she brings up the idea that we try to label these companies as “monopolists” or often bring up the term “privacy” (in regards to our data), but the reality is that we have no real or clear idea as to how much leverage they have over our data, nor how “private” anything really is under these levels of surveillance.

Facebook Got Rid of Racial Ad Categories. Or Did It? By Jon Keegan

After reading the passage from Zuboff’s book, this article was a perfect example of the topic I pointed out in the previous response: companies and corporations taking advertisement data from Facebook users and using it for prejudice; yet refusing to respond clearly to any accusations that these uses of personal data violate both civil rights laws and people’s personal concerns about their data. Unfortunately, and unsurprisingly, there are so many avenues of methods of targeting that it is incredibly easy for the companies to find loopholes around the claim that their targeted advertising could be labeled as “discriminatory.” This article honestly made me more sad, as of course, people of color are mainly targeted in these types of ad categories, specifically given some very interesting keywords in their ad preference data. This article is just a small example of the much larger issue at hand that includes most of the internet and where we browse; who knows how many websites we visit use our data, what they use it for, and how they use it to specifically target us for various different products (products at times that can be very questionable).
