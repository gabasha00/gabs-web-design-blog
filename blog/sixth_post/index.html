Reading Response 5

  • 29 April 2026

The Internet Has Borders by Anna Rose Kerr

Another reading that made me think more about being conscious of my use and understanding what “ethical tech” means to me. Unfortunately, many products we use these days to build our own websites and other forms of tech have some pretty murky investors and/or contribute to movements or ideologies that are not “neutral.” As the author remarks, everything we do online has political weight, whether we feel like it does or not, and I think it is so important to continuously recognize that as developers. Now more than ever we have access to tools that could truly help us “democratise” the web as the author points out, but even then those tools and their ethics come into question. I guess my question at the end of this reading would be, as we’ve discussed in class before, how ethical can anybody truly be using the internet?

I Know a Place by Everest Pipkin

Continuing on: again, there is truly no such thing as a neutral internet. Reading this article made me kinda sad. I used to be an avid roblox user, but not in any recent time; at this point, I frequented it the most between 2010 and 2014. Although the author references their usage to a platform they made around the pandemic, I too also have my own experience with this specific platform and how it used to be so much more of a fun and more “alive” space compared to what it is now. Acquisition of Roblox and the potential of capitalizing off of it for its users is unfortunately just another example of how capitalism and the concept of monetized usage has affected all of us as users and how we interact with the products of users who have/had this idea in mind. I feel like we all just crave community again amongst the over-crowding of these kinds of spaces amongst these platforms.

Alive Internet Theory by Spencer Chang

“The Internet is still a place, but it’s been overdeveloped and undergoverned. Like cities that have prioritized cars over people, visiting the internet now entails controlled apps and search engines, designed for extraction.”

Yeah. Again, a continued theme amongst these three articles. Monetization, profit, and engagement have all heavily shaped the development of many platforms online within the past decade or so. I feel like this reading was a good thing to read last, as it basically summarizes the previous two articles in a much more literal and direct way. I like that the author poses the question to the reader of how we can make the “open internet” more resilient and prevalent amongst the sea of the “dead internet.”

On another note, the concept of the dead internet really scares me as I have now witnessed this happening within the past couple of months. I use TikTok pretty often, and the amount of bots that very accurately emulate what real people's accounts look like is very scary.
