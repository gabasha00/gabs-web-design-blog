# Another Network is Possible by April Glaser #

In an age where censorship and ambiguity in truth of reports from major news stations is extremely prevalent, knowing a website like Indymedia had existed is proof to me that for most of the digital age there is, and has been, a full awareness and wanting for a usage of the web that allows for credible and independent news. It’s sad that it has been taken down in the U.S. and other places, but in a way I’m glad it at least kick-started the progression of people who use the internet for reporting independently of news stations, especially “tactical media” types of posts. As the end of the article relays, “Indymedia shouldn’t be replicated — it was nowhere near perfect. But its example reminds us that a better internet is possible, if we are willing to build it.” I think that was a great summary of what exactly Indymedia proved to us; and I do believe that a better internet is possible through collective action.

# Architecting Emergence by Zhenzhen Qi and Yang Wang #
I really appreciated the authors’ approach to relating systems back to human biology. As a developer, I’ve always looked for approaches to web and program building that are not set into one kind of structured model of typical web design, and allow for contribution, growth, etc. This article really resonated with me by the way in which it explains the idea of how we are basically all connected, all influenced, and all contributors within the same “system” which is life. We can connect all ideas to each other, and in relation to web design, I think it’s crucial to be able to understand this concept as it helps to work with others.

# The woman who taught internet strangers to actually care for one another by Claire L. Evans #

I like that this reading highlighted the importance of active moderation in online communities. As a consistent reddit user, as annoying as some active group moderators can occasionally be…their presence is crucial. It reminds us that at the end of the day, our internet experiences are shaped by us, the user, and our interactions with other users: whether that be indirectly through contributed articles, posts, websites, etc., or directly through chat, video, whatever it may be. Negativity and harmful content and interaction detracts us from use, and I can’t help but think about the fact that, as the author points out, once upon a time there was a more “utopian” outlook and usage, where moderators of most sites just had to make sure that people were interacting in constructive, positive ways. Now moderators are subjected to tons of NSFW and illegal content which I’m sure is not fun (I read somewhere that some moderators are located in third world countries and are paid basically scraps for going through so much of this type of content, unfortunately).
